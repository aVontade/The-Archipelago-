The three-way split: the deportation of Muskien ≈1966/67 tore the family across three countries — father + Abdul + Unusse + Abiba (+ later Laura) in Maputo; mother with the younger children in Johannesburg (Sophiatown → W.N.T. → Westbury); Laura carried to Maputo as Abiba's child, then to Portugal. The brothers were trapped in Mozambique until the Carnation Revolution (1974) freed them (independence 25 June 1975). Of the seven, only Hamede and Laura are living.
The crew (W.N.T. friends): Georgie Jones — 616 Kanyile St neighbour, friend to this day, still alive. Phillip Winnaar — best man at the wedding, jail → Jehovah's Witness, d. ≈2022–23; his transformation began with a forgotten letter Hamede wrote him from Ennerdale. "It would be a grave error to mention my past without mentioning Georgie." — Hamede
The Nels (mother's family): white in appearance, classified "coloured" by apartheid — "racist wannabes" performing provisional whiteness. Siblings at the same station would not greet each other. "That is what apartheid did — it twisted and altered a person's identity." Grandmother: Olive. Alice's sister Julia — loved dearly — married a man the machine broke: he parked his car behind a convent and gassed himself. Years later a cousin and his girlfriend died the same way. "They had many tragedies." Jennifer (Sully's wife) looked white and worked as a white hairdressing specialist.
